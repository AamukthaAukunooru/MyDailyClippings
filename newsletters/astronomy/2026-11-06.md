## Unveiling the Universe's Radio Secrets

Astronomers have just delivered the most exquisitely detailed and sensitive view of the low-frequency radio sky ever achieved. An international team, spearheaded by researchers at Leiden University, utilized the **Low Frequency Array (LOFAR)**, a vast network of radio telescopes, to create an image that reveals an astonishing 630,000 radio sources across the cosmos. This monumental undertaking allows us to observe phenomena that are otherwise invisible to optical telescopes, such as the faint radio emissions from distant galaxies, the remnants of ancient supernovae, and the intricate webs of gas and dust that permeate intergalactic space. The sheer scale of this new image, capturing such a massive number of sources with unprecedented clarity, provides a treasure trove of data for scientists to explore. It's like upgrading from a blurry, black-and-white sketch of the night sky to a vibrant, high-definition panorama, offering fresh insights into the evolution of galaxies and the fundamental processes that shape our universe.

## Powering Up Our Orbital Outpost

A significant milestone was achieved aboard the International Space Station (ISS) this week, as spacewalking astronauts successfully completed the installation of the final **ISS Roll-Out Solar Array (iROSA)**. Astronauts Anil Menon and Joshua Kutryk ventured outside the station on Thursday, November 5, to finish the multiyear effort. This crucial upgrade is set to boost the station's power generation capacity by a remarkable 30 percent. Imagine the difference that extra power makes: it allows for more scientific experiments, supports new technologies, and ensures the continued robust operation of the orbiting laboratory for years to come. The iROSAs are a testament to innovative engineering, designed to be more compact and efficient than the station's original solar panels. Each array, when fully deployed, is a marvel of engineering, unfurling like a giant scroll to capture the sun's energy. This completion not only secures the ISS's future operational capabilities but also demonstrates the incredible precision and teamwork required for human spaceflight and maintenance in the challenging environment of low Earth orbit.
