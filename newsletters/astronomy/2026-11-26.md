## Hera Reveals DART's Asteroid Impact Crater

The European Space Agency's **Hera spacecraft** has delivered humanity's first up-close look at the aftermath of NASA's **Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART)** mission, revealing a "stadium-sized hole" punched into the asteroid Dimorphos. This incredible feat of planetary defense, which occurred over two years ago, saw the DART spacecraft intentionally collide with Dimorphos, a moonlet orbiting the larger asteroid Didymos, to alter its trajectory. Hera's new images provide crucial data on the impact's scale and the material ejected from the asteroid.

The DART mission, a collaborative effort by NASA, aimed to test a kinetic impactor technique to defend Earth from potential asteroid threats. The impact successfully shortened Dimorphos' orbital period around Didymos by 33 minutes, far exceeding predictions. Hera, which arrived at the Didymos binary asteroid system on November 25, 2026, is now meticulously surveying the impact site. The spacecraft even captured a solar eclipse in deep space, as the smaller Dimorphos moonlet cast its shadow across the larger Didymos. This detailed examination by Hera will help scientists understand the asteroid's composition and structure, refining our models for future planetary defense strategies. The sheer scale of the crater, roughly the size of a sports stadium, underscores the power of the impact and the success of this pioneering mission.

## Historic International Visit to Tiangong Space Station

In a landmark moment for international space cooperation, Pakistan's **Muhammad Zeeshan Ali** became the first international astronaut to visit China's **Tiangong space station**. Ali, along with his two Chinese crewmates aboard the **Shenzhou-24** spacecraft, successfully docked with the orbiting outpost on November 25, 2026. This mission marks a significant step in China's burgeoning space program and its efforts to foster international partnerships in human spaceflight.

The Tiangong space station, which has been steadily expanding since its first module launched in 2021, is designed to be a permanent human presence in low Earth orbit. The addition of international astronauts like Muhammad Zeeshan Ali highlights China's commitment to opening its space facilities to global collaboration. This mission not only strengthens ties between Pakistan and China but also broadens the scope of scientific research that can be conducted aboard Tiangong, bringing diverse perspectives and expertise to humanity's endeavors in space. Ali's presence on the station is a powerful symbol of the increasingly global nature of space exploration.

## Webb Peers Back to the Universe's First 500 Million Years

The **James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)** continues to redefine our understanding of the early universe, with an international team of researchers announcing the discovery of the farthest **"little red dot"** to date. These faint, supermassive black holes, first identified in 2022, are crucial clues to how the earliest galaxies and their central black holes formed and evolved. This newly found "little red dot" existed within the universe's first 500 million years, an era known as the **Cosmic Dawn**.

The incredible sensitivity of JWST allows astronomers to peer back in time to when the universe was just a fraction of its current age, observing light that has traveled for over 13 billion years. These "little red dots" are thought to be nascent supermassive black holes, perhaps still shrouded in dust and gas, actively accreting matter and growing rapidly. Their existence so early in cosmic history challenges some existing models of black hole formation, suggesting that these colossal objects may have grown much faster than previously thought. This discovery, pushing the boundaries of observable cosmology, provides invaluable data for scientists striving to unravel the mysteries of the universe's infancy and the origins of the structures we see today.
