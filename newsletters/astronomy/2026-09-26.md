## The Future Takes Flight: Collaboration and Innovation

The future of space exploration is increasingly a global and technologically advanced endeavor. The Republic of San Marino recently became the 76th signatory to the Artemis Accords, a set of non-binding principles for peaceful and cooperative lunar exploration established by NASA and the U.S. Department of State in 2020. This growing international coalition underscores a shared vision for returning humanity to the Moon and establishing a sustainable presence there. A key aspect of future lunar missions will be resource utilization, and new estimates suggest there could be enough water on the Moon to support smaller villages or towns, though perhaps not sprawling metropolises. Such water would be vital for drinking, oxygen production, and even rocket fuel.

Meanwhile, NASA is pushing the boundaries of spacecraft propulsion with tests of a "dual mode propulsion" CubeSat. Traditional spacecraft often rely on bulky, separate systems for different types of maneuvering, but this new technology aims to combine chemical and electric propulsion from a common fuel tank, making future missions more efficient and capable. Adding to the innovation, Stanford engineers are developing AI systems that teach spacecraft to "dream" their way to the International Space Station. This advanced AI allows spacecraft to simulate and learn complex docking maneuvers through "mental simulations," a task equivalent to parallel parking at orbital speeds of 28,000 kilometers per hour. This paves the way for greater autonomy and safety in space operations, with the AI model requiring significantly fewer iterations to master docking compared to traditional reinforcement learning systems.
