A significant escalation in the **Middle East** has seen **Iran** outline peace terms amidst intensified **Houthi** attacks on **Saudi Arabia**, prompting an offer of support from **Turkiye**. Meanwhile, a controversial pardon in **Israel** has reignited debates over military conduct. In **Eastern Europe**, **Ukraine** continues to fortify its vital railway infrastructure against persistent attacks.

## Middle East Escalation and Diplomacy

The conflict in the **Middle East** has seen a notable intensification, with **Houthi** forces claiming responsibility for ballistic missile and drone attacks targeting **Saudi Arabia**'s capital, **Riyadh**, and its oil facilities. A reported strike on a fuel depot at **Riyadh** airport caused delays on Saturday, and **Saudi Arabia** confirmed it had shot down a ballistic missile aimed at the capital. The **Saudi-led coalition** stated its defenses intercepted the missile. This marks the first air raid alert in **Riyadh** since an escalation in fighting between the **Houthis** in **Yemen** and **Saudi Arabia**.

In response to the escalating attacks, **Turkiye** has expressed its backing for **Saudi Arabia**'s security. The **Turkish** Foreign Minister highlighted **Turkiye**'s readiness to assist **Saudi Arabia** militarily. This offer underscores growing regional concern over the conflict's trajectory.

Amidst these developments, **Iran**, a key regional actor, has reportedly set terms for peace. The **Iranian** security chief indicated that Tehran's demands include an end to the war on all fronts and the lifting of the **United States**' naval blockade. The **Houthis** have been described as growing in power and significance, with some analyses suggesting their rising importance in what is termed **Iran**'s "axis of resistance," potentially surpassing the role previously held by **Lebanon**'s **Hezbollah**. This shift highlights the evolving dynamics of proxy warfare in the region.

## Israeli Pardon and Domestic Debate

In **Israel**, President **Isaac Herzog** has pardoned former soldier **Elor Azaria**, who was convicted in 2017 for the manslaughter of an incapacitated **Palestinian** attacker in 2016. The incident, where **Azaria** shot a **Palestinian** who had already been wounded and incapacitated after a stabbing attack, sparked widespread public debate both domestically and internationally over **Israeli** military actions and ethics. The pardon has reignited discussions surrounding the conduct of soldiers in conflict situations and the legal and moral implications of such actions.

## Ukraine's Infrastructure Resilience

In **Eastern Europe**, **Ukraine** is intensifying its efforts to protect its railway infrastructure as **Russia** increases its attacks on these vital networks. The **Ukrainian** government has appealed for international assistance to replace destroyed locomotives and is implementing new plans to ensure the safety of passengers. The railway system remains a crucial artery for both civilian transport and military logistics in **Ukraine**, making its protection a strategic priority amidst the ongoing conflict.

The **Houthis** also claimed to have targeted **Aramco** facilities in **Yanbu**.

The **Turkish** Foreign Minister, **Hakan Fidan**, highlighted **Turkiye**'s readiness to assist **Saudi Arabia** militarily, particularly on technical matters, under the framework of the **Mecca Joint Defense Agreement** signed with **Pakistan** in August. **Fidan** emphasized that **Ankara** views the attacks as a serious threat to **Saudi Arabia**'s territorial integrity and sovereignty.

Amidst these developments, **Iran**, a key regional actor, has reportedly set terms for peace. **Iran**'s Supreme National Security Council Secretary, **Mohsen Rezaei**, stated on Saturday, September 19, that **Tehran**'s demands for negotiations with **Washington** include an end to the war on all fronts, the release of seized **Iranian** assets, and the lifting of the **United States**' naval blockade. **Rezaei** indicated that **Tehran** is awaiting a response from **US President Trump**. The **US** naval blockade on **Iran** was imposed in April 2026, following the failure of earlier talks to end the conflict. Analysts suggest the **Houthis** have become a more powerful and effective ally for **Iran** than **Hezbollah**, having seized **Yemen**'s capital in 2014 and gaining a strategic position on the **Red Sea** at the **Bab el-Mandeb** Strait. Their recent advance on the **Red Sea** coast has strengthened **Tehran**'s leverage in its confrontation with **Washington**.

## Israeli Pardon and Domestic Debate

In **Israel**, President **Isaac Herzog** has pardoned former combat medic **Elor Azaria**, who was convicted in 2017 for the manslaughter of an incapacitated **Palestinian** attacker, **Abdel Fattah al-Sharif**, in **Hebron** in March 2016. The incident involved **Azaria** shooting **al-Sharif** in the head as he lay wounded on the ground after having stabbed an **Israeli** soldier. A video of the shooting circulated widely and sparked intense public debate in **Israel** and internationally regarding military conduct. **Azaria** was sentenced to 18 months in prison, of which he served nine months.

President **Herzog**'s decision, announced on Saturday, September 19, was made upon the recommendation of Defense Minister **Israel Katz** and considered the time that had passed, the spirit of the approaching **Yom Kippur** holiday, and **Azaria**'s expressed regret in his request. However, the military's chief of staff reportedly opposed granting the pardon. The case deeply divided **Israeli** society at the time of the conviction, with many supporting **Azaria** while others, including top generals, pressed for his prosecution. **Israel**'s hard-line Minister of National Security, **Itamar Ben-Gvir**, welcomed the pardon, while **Palestinians** and human rights groups had previously described **Azaria**'s original sentence as far too lenient. The President's office stated that the decision does not constitute a reassessment of the case.

## Ukraine's Infrastructure Resilience

In **Eastern Europe**, **Ukraine** is intensifying its efforts to protect its critical railway infrastructure as **Russia** steps up its drone attacks targeting these networks. According to **Mykola Kalashnyk**, **Ukraine**'s Minister for Restoration, Infrastructure and Transport, **Russia** has attacked **Ukraine**'s railway infrastructure at least 1,534 times since the beginning of 2026.

To mitigate casualties and maintain operations, **Ukraine** has introduced new safety protocols for railway operations during air threats. These measures include suspending passenger boarding and disembarkation during a "red" threat level and directing passengers and staff to the nearest shelters. Decisions regarding trains already in transit will be made on a case-by-case basis, taking into account the operational situation and air threat information. **Ukraine** has also called on Western partners to supply locomotives, as **Russian** attacks have damaged or destroyed over 500 since the start of the war, with most occurring this year alone.
