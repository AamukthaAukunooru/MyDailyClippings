## Regional Shifts: Post-Brexit Europe and African Economic Autonomy

In a significant development for post-Brexit relations, the **European Union** and the **United Kingdom** have reached what has been described as a "landmark deal" aimed at strengthening ties. The agreement, concluded on November 20, reportedly covers areas such as youth mobility, trade in food and agricultural products, and cooperation on emissions trading. **British Prime Minister Andy Burnham's** office stated that both sides agreed on timelines for implementing a youth mobility scheme, a sanitary and phytosanitary agreement, a food export deal, and a link between their emissions trading systems. The youth mobility scheme could allow up to 100,000 young people under 31 from each side to live, work, and study for up to three years. This agreement signals an effort by both sides to reset their relationship and foster greater cooperation.

On the **African** continent, a new credit rating agency has been launched with the ambition of transforming how the continent's economies are assessed. The **Africa Credit Rating Agency (AfCRA)** was officially launched in Port Louis, **Mauritius**, on October 7, 2026, as an **African Union** (AU)-mandated institution. **AfCRA** aims to provide independent, credible, **Africa**-focused assessments of creditworthiness, complementing existing international agencies by drawing on local data and expertise. Its mission is to strengthen **Africa's** financial architecture, close information gaps, and ensure that **African** sovereigns, sub-sovereigns, and businesses are assessed fairly in international financial markets. The agency's interim chief executive, **Sifiso Falala**, stated that **AfCRA** is in discussions with 15 **African** governments, with its first sovereign ratings expected by the end of March 2027.

Politically, **South Africa's African National Congress (ANC)** is reportedly fighting to retain control of key cities following recent local government election setbacks on November 4, 2026. The **ANC** received approximately 30.75 percent of the national vote, a significant decrease from its 2021 local election result of 45.59 percent. The **Democratic Alliance (DA)** secured around 24.65 percent of the vote. The **ANC** appears hesitant to replicate its national power-sharing arrangement with the pro-business **DA** at the local level. This struggle for municipal control highlights ongoing political realignments within **South Africa** and could have implications for governance and service delivery in major urban centers.
