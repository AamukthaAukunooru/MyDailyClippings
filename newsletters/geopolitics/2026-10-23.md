My Daily Clippings

Today's geopolitical landscape is marked by escalating tensions in the Middle East, significant political developments and unrest across Europe, and ongoing humanitarian and security challenges in Asia and Africa. A powerful earthquake has struck **Tibet**, while the **Korean Peninsula** faces a renewed propaganda standoff. Meanwhile, **Serbia** is on the cusp of a pivotal election, and protests continue in **Albania** and **France**.

## Middle East Tensions and US Policy

Tensions in the Middle East remain high, with **US President Donald Trump** indicating the possibility of new strikes on **Iran**. **Trump** stated on Thursday that the **US** has already "knocked the hell out" of **Iran** and suggested further action might be taken. These remarks come amidst reports that the **US** and **Israel** are developing plans for renewed strikes on **Iran** following the **US** midterm elections on November 3.

**Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu** has openly called for the overthrow of the **Iranian** regime, describing it as an "existential threat". **Netanyahu** has instructed his military and intelligence leaders to pursue this objective. He also stated that he would not seek a "green light" from **President Trump** for such actions but would brief him on **Israel's** intentions. Potential targets for these strikes reportedly include **Iranian** energy infrastructure, nuclear facilities, and arsenals of missiles and drones. In response, **Iranian** officials have warned that any new strikes could lead to retaliation, with targets potentially expanding to include data centers and undersea internet cables.

## European Political Dynamics and Social Unrest

Across Europe, several nations are grappling with significant political events and social unrest. In **Serbia**, a pivotal parliamentary election is scheduled for Sunday, October 25. A large student-led rally in **Belgrade** on Thursday aimed to challenge the party of **President Aleksandar Vucic**, who has been a dominant political figure since 2012. **Vucic** recently resigned as president to lead the **Serbian Progressive Party (SNS)** list in the election, seeking to become prime minister. The **Student List** movement emerged from nearly two years of anti-corruption protests, sparked by the 2024 collapse of a railway station canopy in Novi Sad that resulted in sixteen fatalities.

Meanwhile, **Albania** continues to experience growing student-led anti-corruption protests, which began on October 13. Demonstrators are demanding improved education conditions and the resignation of **Prime Minister Edi Rama** amidst allegations of corruption. Clashes have been reported, with government buildings and banks set ablaze, and police employing tear gas, water cannons, and rubber bullets against protesters. On October 22, lawmakers even scuffled in parliament during a session.

In **France**, **President Emmanuel Macron** has vowed to prevent a recurrence of recent urban violence, announcing that "illegitimate" demonstrations will be banned. This decision follows widespread unrest on October 17, which included looting and burning, leading to over 2,500 arrests. **Interior Minister Laurent Nunez** acknowledged that police had "lost control" in some cities during the events. The protests, initially focused on issues like staff shortages and dilapidated school buildings, have broadened to encompass concerns over the high cost of living.

## Developments in Asia and Africa

A powerful 7.0-magnitude earthquake struck the northern part of **Nagqu** in **China's Tibet Autonomous Region** on October 22, reportedly killing at least sixteen people and injuring 46 others. The quake, which hit a mountainous and sparsely populated area, also caused the collapse of at least 339 homes.

On the **Korean Peninsula**, tensions are rising as **North Korea** prepares to resume propaganda loudspeaker broadcasts along its entire border with **South Korea** within days. **Kim Yo-jong**, the influential sister of **North Korean leader Kim Jong-un**, stated that the loudspeakers would be operational by the end of October. This move follows increased animosity, including **South Korean** warning shots and broadcasts in response to **North Korean** border fortification efforts and alleged incursions. If activated, these would mark the first loudspeaker exchanges in sixteen months, as **South Korea** had previously suspended its broadcasts in June 2025 to de-escalate tensions. **Pyongyang** has also threatened to deploy additional tactical strike capabilities and "mercilessly eliminate" **South Korean** forces if warning fire causes harm.

In **Sudan**, a **BBC** investigation has revealed that hospitals have been deliberately targeted by **Rapid Support Forces (RSF)** fighters. **UN** human rights chief **Volker Türk** indicated that these multiple attacks may constitute war crimes. Reports from **Insecurity Insight** document over 800 attacks on healthcare since April 2023, with more than 55 percent attributed to the **RSF**, including damage to 229 facilities and the killing of 222 health workers.

In **East Africa**, hundreds of **Ethiopians** are seeking refuge in **Kenya**, fleeing regional fighting in the **Moyale** town area, which is shared administratively by **Ethiopia's Oromia** and **Somali** regions. The conflict, which escalated into heavy gunfire on October 22, originated from a dispute over the raising of a **Somali Regional State** flag and the killing of a thirteen-year-old girl on October 18. The **Ethiopian National Defence Force (ENDF)** and Federal Police have reportedly not intervened in the ongoing clashes.

Finally, **US President Donald Trump** announced that **Elon Musk** has restored **Ukraine's Starlink** access. This development follows earlier reports of restrictions on **Ukraine's Starlink** service, which is vital for battlefield communications and drone operations. While **Trump** declined to confirm if he had directly spoken to **Musk**, **Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy** had previously noted that Starlink speeds had not been fully restored and that the restrictions had disrupted military missions.
