## US Foreign Policy and Global Realignments

**United States** foreign policy is undergoing notable shifts, particularly concerning its engagement with **Ukraine** and **Iran**. **President Donald Trump** has asserted a **US Navy** blockade has halted all **Iranian** ships from traversing the **Strait of Hormuz**. This claim follows a period of heightened tensions in the strait, a critical choke point for global energy trade, which has seen **Iran** restrict passage and the **US** impose a naval blockade on **Iran** since April 2026 after the failure of the Islamabad Talks to end the 2026 **Iran** war. The **US** reinstated the naval blockade in July 2026, with reports indicating it has since halted new **Iranian** oil exports from the **Persian Gulf**. Previously, **President Trump** had also proposed imposing a 20% tariff on all cargoes shipped through the **Strait of Hormuz**, a move that the International Maritime Organization stated had no legal basis.

Concurrently, **US** support for **Ukraine** is facing scrutiny. **Italian Defence Minister Guido Crosetto** criticized **Trump's** restrictions on aid to **Ukraine**, stating it risks prolonging **Russia's** invasion. **Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky** has spoken at an **EU** summit amidst these tensions, with **EU** leaders reaffirming their support for **Ukraine**. Reports indicate a significant decrease in **US** military support for **Ukraine** during **President Trump's** second term, with **US** allocations for Fiscal Year 2026 dropping by 99% compared to peak years of the conflict. This reduction in aid has led to concerns about **Ukraine's** dwindling stock of **US**-made Patriot interceptor missiles. **President Trump** has also publicly expressed skepticism about **President Zelensky**, stating he is "not a good guy" and suggesting he does not genuinely seek a settlement to the conflict. This stance contrasts with the previous **Biden** administration's emphasis on continued financial and military assistance. The concept of an "axis of upheaval," comprising **Russia**, **China**, **Iran**, and **North Korea**, is also gaining analytical traction, with experts suggesting these nations are deepening their cooperation, driven by shared opposition to a **US**-led global order. This alignment is seen as fundamentally altering the geopolitical landscape and increasing the military capabilities of these revisionist actors.

## Regional Instability and Internal Dynamics

Across different regions, internal and localized conflicts continue to pose significant challenges. In **Albania**, student protests against corruption and poor university conditions have intensified, leading to clashes with riot police. **Albanian Prime Minister Edi Rama** has attributed the unrest to "foreign hands". The protests, which have seen students demand political change and **Prime Minister Rama's** resignation, have escalated to include property damage and violent confrontations with authorities. The "Flamingo movement," as it's known, has also voiced opposition to a luxury tourism development project on **Albania's** southern coast linked to **Jared Kushner**, **President Trump's** son-in-law.

In the **West Bank**, **Israeli** settlers have launched coordinated attacks on **Palestinians**, particularly disrupting the annual olive harvest. These incidents have resulted in injuries to **Palestinians** and a **Washington Post** photojournalist, with reports of burning cars and trees. **Israel's** military has condemned the violence and acknowledged the injury of a journalist, though residents and rights groups suggest authorities have done little to stop the attacks, which have reportedly soared this year. Separately, **Italy** is probing **Israel's Katz** over allegations of abuse and sexual assault of flotilla activists.

**Venezuela** is navigating complex internal and international legal issues. The **Venezuelan** opposition leader, **María Corina Machado**, has accused **President Trump's** new oil partner in **Venezuela** of corruption. Furthermore, **Venezuela** formally announced its withdrawal from the **International Criminal Court (ICC)** in July 2026, a decision it described as "firm and irrevocable". **Venezuelan** officials cited a "geographical bias" of the **ICC**, claiming it unfairly focuses on countries in **Africa** and **Latin America**. The **ICC** had opened an investigation into alleged crimes against humanity in **Venezuela** in 2021, relating to a 2017 crackdown on protesters. Despite the withdrawal, which takes effect one year after notification, the **ICC** can still investigate and prosecute crimes that occurred while **Venezuela** was a member. The **US** State Department has reportedly praised **Venezuela's** move, aligning with a broader **US** campaign to challenge the **ICC's** authority.
