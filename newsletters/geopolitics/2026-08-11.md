## The Americas Respond to Crisis and Policy Shifts

**Colombia** was struck by a powerful 7.4 magnitude earthquake on Monday, leading to widespread damage and a rising death toll. The quake, which shook major cities including **Bogotá**, **Cali**, and **Medellín**, has resulted in at least 111 fatalities, a number expected to increase as rescue efforts continue. The Pacific Coast region near the epicenter sustained major damage, with more than a dozen buildings collapsing. This natural disaster has presented an immediate and significant challenge for **Colombia's** newly inaugurated President **Abelardo De La Espriella**, who only took office on Friday. President **De La Espriella** has announced his leadership of emergency rescue efforts, particularly in the earthquake-struck areas. Video footage from **Pereira** airport captured the violent shaking during the temblor, underscoring the intensity of the event. Rescue teams are actively searching collapsed buildings, with people reportedly trapped in cities like **Cali**.

In the **United States**, President **Donald Trump** signed an executive order on Monday that aims to significantly alter the recommended childhood vaccine schedule. The order specifically recommends separating the measles, mumps, and rubella (**MMR**) vaccine into three distinct shots, administered during separate visits. Furthermore, the administration stated it would no longer recommend hepatitis B, COVID-19, and influenza shots for children. During the signing, President **Trump** and Health Secretary **RFK Jr.** reportedly referenced autism, despite numerous studies showing no link between vaccines and autism. This executive order signals a sweeping change to public health policy and has sparked considerable debate.

## Shifting Geopolitical Landscapes in Eurasia

**Russia's** political landscape has seen a further constriction of opposition voices, as the **Yabloko** party, the country's only liberal anti-war party, was barred from participating in upcoming parliamentary elections. A **Moscow** court ruled against their participation, effectively preventing them from standing in next month's elections. **Yabloko** has historically attempted to navigate the increasingly restrictive political environment under the **Kremlin**, but its pro-peace manifesto ultimately proved to be a "red line". This decision highlights the ongoing suppression of dissent and independent political activity within **Russia**.

Meanwhile, **Turkey's** parliament has approved a historic law aimed at reintegrating **PKK** militants, marking a significant step towards potentially ending a four-decade-long conflict. The new legislation outlines a framework for some militants to return to civilian life, though the future of **Abdullah Ocalan**, the imprisoned leader of the **PKK**, remains unresolved. This legislative move represents a notable effort by the **Turkish** government to address a deeply entrenched internal conflict.

In a broader regional development, a new security alliance, dubbed the **Mecca Pact**, has been formally established between **Saudi Arabia**, **Turkey**, and **Pakistan**. Discussions leading to this trilateral agreement had been ongoing for six months. Observers suggest that the pact's primary objective is not to create a mutual defense commitment where countries fight for each other, but rather to prevent regional dominance by other powers, specifically **Iran** and **Israel**. This agreement could reshape security dynamics in the wider **Middle East** and **South Asia**, extending **Pakistan's** security role westward.
