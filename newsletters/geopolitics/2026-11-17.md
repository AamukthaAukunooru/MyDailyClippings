A notable trilateral meeting between the leaders of **China**, the **United States**, and **Russia** is set to take place, while tensions persist in the **Middle East** and the conflict in **Ukraine** continues to escalate. Meanwhile, several **European Union** nations have announced a new, hardened approach to migration by establishing a deportation hub in **Rwanda**.

## Global Power Dynamics and Tech Rivalry

A historic trilateral summit is scheduled for Thursday in **Shenzhen**, **China**, bringing together **Chinese President Xi Jinping**, **U.S. President Donald Trump**, and **Russian President Vladimir Putin**. **China's** foreign ministry stated the leaders would engage in "in-depth exchanges on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability in major-power relations," describing the meeting as being of "groundbreaking significance." While **Beijing** has not confirmed specific agenda items, many observers, including the **UN** Secretary-General, hope for discussions on denuclearization among the world's three largest nuclear powers. Reports indicate that **President Trump** aims to focus on nuclear non-proliferation, while **President Putin** seeks an agreement on **Ukraine** reflecting **Moscow's** terms. **China**, however, has reportedly been wary of including **Ukraine** on the agenda and has shown little interest in nuclear arms control talks with the **U.S.** and **Russia**, viewing its nuclear arsenal as significantly smaller than theirs. Some analysts suggest that **Putin** and **Xi** may perceive a weakened **Trump** in these discussions.

In related developments, the intensifying AI race between the **U.S.** and **China** continues to be a focal point, particularly concerning advanced chip technology. **President Trump** has defended Commerce Secretary **Howard Lutnick** amidst allegations of slow-walking export restrictions on **Nvidia** chips to **China**. Treasury Secretary **Scott Bessent** publicly criticized the Commerce Department for delays in adding **Singapore**-based **Megaspeed**, a company linked to a suspected **Chinese** intelligence operation and a major buyer of **Nvidia** chips, to a U.S. blacklist. The Commerce Department has since issued an interim final rule to close a loophole that allowed **China** to remotely access advanced AI chips through third countries. This scrutiny follows the recent resignation of **Nvidia** CEO **Jensen Huang**, who stepped down amid reports of extramarital affairs, including one with an individual allegedly involved in a **Chinese** spy operation. **Taiwan's** Central Bank Governor **Yang Chin-long** stated that **Huang's** departure is expected to cause only "short-term turbulence" for **Taiwan's** AI suppliers, expressing confidence in the island's role in the global AI supply chain.

## European Security and Migration Policy Shifts

The conflict in **Ukraine** saw a significant escalation with a reported **Ukrainian** drone attack on the **Crimea bridge**, a critical link between **Russia** and the annexed peninsula. **Russia** claimed to have intercepted over two thousand **Ukrainian** drones in a single day, marking what it described as the largest drone attack since the war began. Automobile traffic on the **Crimea bridge** was temporarily restricted, and **Kyiv** experienced a record seventeen air raid alerts. Satellite images reportedly show damage to the bridge deck and a possible deformation of its arch.

Further north, **Estonia** announced plans to close all its border crossings with **Russia** from December 1st, following an attempted arson attack on an **Estonian** drone manufacturer, **Threod Systems**, which **Estonian** authorities attribute to **Russian** military intelligence, the **GRU**. Eight individuals, identified as **Latvian** citizens, were detained in connection with the incident near **Tallinn**. **Estonian** Foreign Minister **Margus Tsahkna** stated that the closure aims to reduce **Russia's** ability to conduct hostile activities and prevent sanctions circumvention. The **Kremlin** has denied involvement, calling the accusations baseless.

In a significant policy shift, five **European Union** countries – **Germany**, **Austria**, the **Netherlands**, **Greece**, and **Denmark** – have announced a plan to establish a "return hub" in **Rwanda** for rejected asylum seekers. This initiative, expected to begin deportations in 2027, represents a hardening of **European** migration policy. The initial facility is projected to accommodate around one hundred people at a time, with migrants expected to integrate into **Rwandan** society or voluntarily return to their countries of origin. The plan has drawn criticism from human rights groups, who express concerns about potential violations of migrants' rights.

## Middle East Tensions

Tensions in the **Middle East** remain high, with ongoing confrontations involving **Iran** and its regional allies. Airstrikes on **Iran's** missile sites have reportedly shed light on the justification for a network of water projects in the country. **Iran's** Revolutionary Guards had previously unveiled a new underground missile site, stating some production had been moved underground to protect it from airstrikes.

In **Iraq**, the army has deployed forces around **Jurf al-Sakhr**, a stronghold of the **Iran**-backed **Kataib Hezbollah** militia. This move comes amid **U.S.** pressure on **Iraq** to curb **Tehran's** influence and follows an investigation into alleged oil smuggling. Iraqi Prime Minister **Ali al-Zaidi** has reportedly given **Kataib Hezbollah** a deadline to relinquish control of the area, raising the potential for direct confrontation.

Meanwhile, in **Bahrain**, a court sentenced dozens of Shiite clerics to life in prison on terrorism charges, accusing them of colluding with **Iran's** Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (**IRGC**). The mass trial involved sixty-nine **Bahraini** Shiites, including sixty clerics, with thirteen tried in absentia. Rights groups, such as the **Britain**-based **Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy (BIRD)**, have raised concerns about torture allegations and religious persecution in the case. **Iran's** Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed responsibility for a drone attack on a radar system at **al Riffa Air Base** in **Bahrain** on November fourteenth, stating it was in retaliation for previous **U.S.** strikes on **Iranian** coastal infrastructure.
