## Middle East: Internal Dynamics and Regional Conflicts

Internal divisions within **Iran** are becoming more apparent, highlighted by the conviction and jailing of hard-line lawmaker **Hamid Rasaei**. **Rasaei**, a vocal critic of a diplomatic agreement with the **United States** aimed at ending the war, was found guilty of disseminating false information with the intent to harm the Parliament speaker. This development underscores existing "deep rifts in its leadership". Simultaneously, reports indicate that the **Iranian** government is actively working to suppress dissent, with accusations of blocking SIM cards, bank access, and online accounts for individuals who have posted critical content or engaged in activism.

The broader regional impact of **Iran's** activities is also evident. This ongoing conflict continues to draw international attention and concern. These charges highlight concerns about the international reach of groups or individuals linked to **Iran's** foreign policy objectives.

The fragile 2022 peace deal has reportedly collapsed, and international mediators have struggled to bring the warring sides to the negotiation table. Foreign powers, including **Eritrea**, **Egypt**, the **United Arab Emirates**, and **Saudi Arabia**, are reportedly involved, further complicating efforts to de-escalate the situation. The conflict is now considered **Ethiopia's** most complex armed conflict in contemporary times.

## East Asia and US Foreign Policy

Tensions on the **Korean Peninsula** remain high following a ballistic missile launch by **North Korea**. The missile was fired towards the sea from the port city of Wonsan, according to **South Korea's** military. This action follows accusations traded between **North** and **South Korea** regarding landmines, after an incident injured three **South Korean** soldiers, renewing scrutiny of **North Korea's** actions along the border.

Meanwhile, **United States** foreign policy under **President Trump** continues to exert pressure on allies. **President Trump** has threatened to double tariffs on **South Korea** if the nation does not agree to pay for an **Alaska** natural gas pipeline project. This threat follows a previous agreement last year where tariffs on imports from **South Korea** were reportedly lowered in exchange for billions of dollars in investments in the **US**. The proposed **Alaska** natural gas pipeline aims to transport natural gas from Alaska’s North Slope to a liquefaction plant on the state’s southern coast for export, primarily to Asian markets.
