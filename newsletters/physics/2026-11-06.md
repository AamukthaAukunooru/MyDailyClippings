Welcome to My Daily Clippings, your regular dive into the most compelling physics discoveries shaping our understanding of the universe. This week has delivered a remarkable array of breakthroughs, from peering into the primordial soup of the early cosmos and confirming elusive particle decays, to uncovering a brand new form of magnetism and manipulating exotic quantum states. These advancements underscore the relentless pace of discovery at the frontiers of fundamental science and materials research.

## Probing the Universe's Earliest Moments and Rarest Decays

Our journey begins at the very dawn of time, with physicists making strides in understanding the **quark-gluon plasma**, the universe's first fluid. In the first fraction of a second after the Big Bang, matter existed as this incredibly hot, trillion-degree plasma of quarks and gluons, zipping around at nearly the speed of light. New research published on November fifth is helping to pin down the intricate structure of this primordial soup, offering crucial insights into the conditions that prevailed when the cosmos was just beginning to form.

Meanwhile, at the other end of the cosmic scale, the colossal experiments at CERN have finally yielded evidence for an **ultrarare particle decay** that physicists have been hunting for four decades. On November fifth, researchers reported detecting the decay of a **B⁰ meson** into a muon and an antimuon (B⁰→ µ⁺µ⁻). This incredibly infrequent event occurs when the meson's beauty quark annihilates with a down-type quark. The observation of this decay, which aligns with predictions from the Standard Model of particle physics, provides a powerful validation of our current understanding of fundamental forces and particles, while also setting stringent limits on potential new physics beyond the Standard Model.

Adding another fascinating dimension to our exploration of extreme phenomena, scientists have also created an experimental platform to simulate **black hole properties** using ultracold atoms. Published on November fourth in *APS Physics Magazine*, this innovative approach uses sheets of light to corral waves of ultracold atoms, effectively creating an analog for the event horizons of black holes. This allows researchers to study the complex physics of these enigmatic cosmic objects in a controlled laboratory setting, potentially revealing new insights into gravity and quantum mechanics.

## Unveiling New Forms of Magnetism and Quantum Control

In the realm of condensed matter physics, a groundbreaking discovery has challenged nearly ninety years of conventional wisdom regarding magnetism. For decades, scientists believed magnetism fell into just two categories: ferromagnetism, like the magnets on your fridge, and antiferromagnetism, where magnetic moments cancel out. However, on November fourth, SLAC scientists announced conclusive proof of a remarkable **third type of magnetism** they've dubbed **altermagnetism**. Using specialized X-rays, they uncovered a unique magnetic order that combines aspects of both ferromagnetism and antiferromagnetism, opening up entirely new avenues for research in spintronics and advanced materials. This discovery, published in *ScienceDaily*, has the potential to revolutionize how we design and utilize magnetic materials in future technologies.

Further pushing the boundaries of quantum control, researchers at Tohoku University and the Japan Atomic Energy Agency (JAEA) have successfully demonstrated the **electrical control of an unconventional quantum transport state**. On November fifth, they reported the first-ever successful manipulation of the **quantum-geometric nonlinear Hall (QgNLH) state** using electric current. This unique quantum phenomenon, previously observed but difficult to control, holds immense promise for developing novel electronic devices and quantum computing architectures. The ability to precisely tune such exotic quantum states electrically represents a significant step forward in harnessing quantum mechanics for practical applications.
