## Quantum Leaps and Bounds

The quest to build powerful **quantum computers** and simulators continues its relentless pace, with researchers pushing the boundaries of coherence and control. Scientists at the 5th Institute of Physics of the University of Stuttgart have recently set three new records for the stability of **circular Rydberg atoms**, keeping them stable for an impressive 11 milliseconds. These highly excited atoms, with electrons orbiting at large distances from the nucleus, are considered promising candidates for quantum information processing due to their strong interactions and long coherence times. This achievement marks a significant step toward developing more robust and scalable quantum systems.

Meanwhile, the practicalities of a future quantum economy are also being meticulously examined. A recent study, highlighted in *Physics Magazine*, delves into the **reliability of quantum batteries**. Marric Stephens reports that increasing the charging rate of a quantum battery inherently leads to unavoidable fluctuations in the total energy stored. This finding is crucial for understanding the trade-offs involved in designing and operating quantum energy storage devices, suggesting that there's a fundamental limit to how quickly and precisely energy can be stored in these novel systems.

## Adventures in Antimatter and Aperiodic Physics

In a truly remarkable feat of experimental physics, the **BASE collaboration** achieved a world first in March 2026: the successful road transport of **antiprotons**. Using a specially designed mobile trap loaded onto a truck, scientists moved 92 antiprotons and managed to store them for over a month. This unprecedented achievement, realized at the European Organization for Nuclear Research (CERN), opens up exciting new possibilities for antimatter research, potentially allowing experiments to be conducted at facilities away from the large particle accelerators where antimatter is produced. The ability to transport and store antimatter reliably could have profound implications for fundamental physics studies, from testing the symmetries of nature to exploring potential applications.

Shifting gears to a different kind of fundamental discovery, a mathematical shape known for its unique ability to cover a surface without ever repeating, often referred to as the "Einstein problem" in tiling, has revealed some strange new physics. This **aperiodic tiling** has shown an unexpected capacity to twist light into unusual **chiral patterns**. Published in the last 48 hours, this discovery could pave the way for novel methods of controlling light, particularly its polarization, with potential applications in optical devices and advanced materials.

## Engineering Solutions for a Thirsty World

Addressing one of humanity's most pressing environmental challenges, scientists have developed a groundbreaking **solar-powered desalination system** that promises to turn seawater into fresh water without producing harmful brine. This innovative technology tackles the significant environmental problem posed by conventional desalination plants, which typically discharge highly concentrated saltwater brine back into the ocean, harming marine ecosystems. Instead, this new system removes nearly all of the leftover salt as a solid. Described as a self-cleaning technology, this development represents a major step forward in providing sustainable access to clean drinking water globally, especially in arid regions.
