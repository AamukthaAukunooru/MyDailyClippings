My Daily Clippings

This week, the world of mathematics is buzzing with the accelerating influence of artificial intelligence. We're seeing AI models not only tackling problems that have stumped human minds for decades but also prompting a profound re-evaluation of what mathematical discovery truly means. From cracking Millennium Prize Problems to inspiring new collaborative challenges, AI is reshaping the landscape of how we approach and understand the most abstract of sciences.

## AI's Unprecedented Problem-Solving Prowess

Recent months have seen a flurry of announcements highlighting AI's remarkable ability to solve previously intractable mathematical problems, a development that is, understandably, "shaking top-level mathematicians to the core". In a significant breakthrough this past September, **OpenAI** claimed to have solved the **Navier-Stokes problem**, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems established by the Clay Mathematics Institute. This monumental achievement was reportedly accomplished by an internal OpenAI system, more powerful than its GPT-6 Astra model, leveraging the efforts of approximately ten thousand AI agents over eighty-eight hours.

Not to be outdone, **Anthropic's Claude AI** also made waves in August 2026 with its work on the **Riemann Hypothesis**, another Millennium Prize Problem. While not a complete solution, Claude managed to raise the proven share of zeros on the critical line from about forty-one percent to roughly sixty-seven percent, a substantial leap forward. These successes are not isolated incidents. A consortium of leading mathematicians, known as **First Proof**, including **Lauren K. Williams**, a professor at Harvard, recently tested various AI systems against ten challenging research math problems that had only just been solved by humans. The AI systems collectively cracked all ten problems, though they still struggle with clearly explaining their solutions and providing proper citations. These developments are forcing mathematicians to ask fundamental questions about their role in an era where AI can seemingly "solve the unsolvable".

## Math 2.0: AI as a Partner for Deeper Understanding

Amidst the excitement and apprehension surrounding AI's problem-solving capabilities, Fields Medalist **Terence Tao** has proposed a vision for "Math 2.0," where AI serves not as a replacement for human intellect but as a powerful assistant to enhance our understanding. On October twentieth, 2026, Tao launched an **experimental AI-math challenge** on his blog, "What's New," embodying this collaborative spirit.

The challenge focuses on using **tenth discriminant bounds to explore arguments ruling out Siegel zeroes**. To understand why this matters, think of **Siegel zeroes** as hypothetical, elusive exceptions in the complex landscape of prime numbers. Their non-existence has profound implications for how we understand the distribution of primes and is even connected to famous unsolved problems like the **twin prime conjecture**, which posits that there are infinitely many pairs of prime numbers that differ by two.

The challenge specifically asks participants to use AI tools to find the best explicit numerical upper bound for a hypothetical "tenth discriminant." While the classical **class number one problem** in algebraic number theory has already been solved, confirming there are exactly nine such fields, this challenge isn't about re-solving a known problem. Instead, it's designed to encourage rapid, iterative mathematical exploration using AI, pushing the boundaries of how we can leverage these tools to gain new insights and develop novel methods, rather than simply arriving at an answer. Tao emphasizes that the journey of discovery, including the new techniques and unexpected connections found along the way, is often more valuable than the final proof itself. His "Math 2.0" vision seeks to prevent an "era of proof abundance" from inadvertently "sterilizing the surrounding field" by ensuring that human understanding remains central to mathematical progress.
