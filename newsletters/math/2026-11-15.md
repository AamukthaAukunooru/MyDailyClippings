Greetings, fellow travelers on the path of logic and numbers! Today's mathematical landscape is buzzing with the transformative power of artificial intelligence, not just in solving new problems, but also in shedding light on the historical giants of our field. We're seeing AI act as a digital archaeologist, unearthing hidden gems from the past, while simultaneously pushing the boundaries of what's possible in modern mathematical discovery and collaboration.

## AI Unlocks Kurt Gödel's Hidden Mathematical Legacy

In a truly remarkable development, artificial intelligence has helped uncover hundreds of previously unknown theorems and mathematical insights from the unpublished notebooks of one of the 20th century's most profound logicians, Kurt Gödel. Gödel, renowned for his **incompleteness theorems** that fundamentally changed our understanding of mathematics and laid groundwork for the digital age, left behind tens of thousands of pages of unpublished work. Among these are sixteen densely written notebooks from the early 1940s, totaling one thousand nine pages, much of it in an obscure German shorthand called Gabelsberger.

For years, Finnish logician Jan von Plato of Helsinki University led a project to decipher these workbooks, a painstaking process that yielded only about one page of understanding per week. However, in September, von Plato enlisted the help of the AI model **Claude**. Within a mere couple of days, Claude provided a comprehensive digest of the entire material, revealing that Gödel had proven hundreds of theorems that were previously unknown to the mathematical community.

This discovery is not just about historical curiosity; it suggests Gödel was "far ahead of his time," anticipating results that weren't known until the 1970s and 1980s, and even identifying fourteen themes in logic that have yet to be explored. For instance, Gödel had claimed to have proven the independence of the **axiom of choice** decades before American mathematician Paul Cohen won the Fields Medal, mathematics' highest honor, for the same result in 1966. Von Plato believes that had Gödel published this work, he might have won the 1940 Fields Medal. The AI's ability to "connect all the pieces in a thousand pages" and see Gödel's writings as a single, cohesive body of work highlights a new frontier in historical research, allowing us to grasp the full scope of a genius's contributions in ways previously impossible for human researchers.

## AI Accelerates Mathematical Discovery and Collaboration

Beyond historical revelations, artificial intelligence is rapidly reshaping the landscape of contemporary mathematical research, from tackling long-standing problems to fostering new interdisciplinary collaborations. A significant indicator of this acceleration is the recent report that a frontier AI model reportedly solved one of the elusive **Millennium Prize Problems** in approximately eighty-eight hours. These are seven of the most significant unsolved questions in mathematics, each carrying a one million dollar award for a verified solution, with only one, the Poincaré conjecture, having been solved before this recent AI breakthrough. While the full verification process for this AI-generated solution is ongoing, the mere claim underscores the dramatic leap in AI's problem-solving capabilities in verifiable mathematical domains.

This rapid progress has led to a flurry of discussion within the mathematical community, with some, like mathematician Terence Tao, engaging in conversations about a "mathpocalypse" – a term reflecting the profound impact of AI-generated proofs and discoveries. In October, OpenAI released seven hundred twenty-two mathematical manuscripts generated by an internal AI model, claiming significant results across many research areas. This volume of AI-generated content has prompted the creation of new platforms like **MathHelm**, a collaborative space for researchers worldwide to discuss, question, and verify these AI-generated papers. This initiative acknowledges that while AI can generate proofs, human mathematicians remain crucial for understanding, contextualizing, and ensuring the rigor of these new findings.

The integration of AI is also driving major research initiatives. The University of Southern California recently announced a six million dollar grant renewal from the **Simons Foundation** for an international research collaboration. This initiative, directed by USC mathematician Aaron Lauda, brings together eleven institutions to investigate the fascinating intersections of **low-dimensional topology**, **quantum computing**, and **artificial intelligence**. The collaboration, which has now received a total of fourteen million dollars from the Simons Foundation, is actively using AI to resolve complex mathematical challenges, pairing heuristic AI searches with rigorous algebraic reasoning to establish new methods for integrating AI into formal mathematical proof. This kind of interdisciplinary funding highlights a growing recognition that the future of mathematical breakthroughs will increasingly involve leveraging advanced computational tools to explore complex theoretical spaces.

The mathematical community is also adapting to the reality of AI in academic processes. Institutions like the Institute for Advanced Study, the University of Cambridge, and MIT are now asking applicants for postdoctoral and faculty positions to disclose their use of generative AI in their past work and application materials. This reflects a proactive approach to understanding and integrating AI's role while maintaining the core values of originality and deep mathematical understanding in research.
