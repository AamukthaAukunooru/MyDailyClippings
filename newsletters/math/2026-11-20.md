Today's "My Daily Clippings" brings us to a fascinating juncture where artificial intelligence continues to push the boundaries of mathematical discovery, prompting both awe at its capabilities and critical reflection within the human mathematical community. We're seeing AI tackle long-standing problems in theoretical physics and mathematics, while leading mathematicians are actively shaping how we interact with these powerful new tools, ensuring transparency and open access.

## Claude AI Cracks Black Hole Mysteries

In a significant development, Anthropic's **Claude AI** system has independently solved two open theoretical problems in **black hole physics**, a feat announced with the release of its solutions and verification methodology on November eighteenth, two thousand twenty-six. This marks a notable milestone, as it's the first time an AI model has resolved fundamental questions that have stumped human physicists for decades.

The problems tackled by Claude AI involve intricate mathematical relationships concerning **black hole entropy**, **Hawking radiation**, and the preservation of **quantum information**. One problem focused on how information encoded in matter falling into a black hole might theoretically be recovered, a core aspect of the famous **black hole information paradox**. The other involved precisely calculating the thermodynamic equilibrium states for rotating black holes under specific quantum field conditions.

These are not trivial challenges. Previous human attempts often relied on approximations, leaving key aspects unresolved. Claude's approach, however, employed novel computational strategies to identify exact solutions. Anthropic collaborated with theoretical physicists at multiple institutions to independently verify these solutions over a three-month period before their public release. Peer reviewers have acknowledged that Claude's solutions not only satisfy all necessary mathematical constraints but also align with existing physical principles, potentially opening new avenues for research into **quantum gravity** and **black hole mechanics**.

Why does this matter to mathematicians? Beyond the immediate implications for theoretical physics, this demonstrates AI's growing capacity for **deductive reasoning** and **theoretical discovery**, moving beyond mere pattern recognition. It shows that AI can generate entirely new mathematical insights, not just process existing data. This capability challenges our traditional understanding of mathematical intuition and problem-solving, suggesting a future where AI acts as a true partner in fundamental research.

## Mathematicians Build Their Own AI Tools Amidst Growing Concerns

As AI continues its impressive streak in mathematical problem-solving, the human mathematical community is actively engaging with its implications, particularly regarding data handling and the nature of discovery. On November twentieth, two thousand twenty-six, UCLA math professor **Terence Tao**, a Fields Medalist, was featured in an article discussing how researchers are developing alternatives to commercial AI systems.

The concern stems from how mathematical discoveries and research data are managed within proprietary commercial AI models. To address this, Tao recently demonstrated **Open Math OS**, a platform he founded through a nonprofit. This initiative aims to provide access to **open-source AI models**, cloud computing, and collaborative tools specifically tailored for mathematical research. The goal is to create an environment where mathematicians have greater control and transparency over the tools they use, fostering a more open and verifiable approach to AI-assisted discovery.

This movement reflects a broader conversation within the mathematical world about the role of AI. For instance, the "Futures of Maths" platform, launched on November fifth, two thousand twenty-six, serves as a living forum for active research mathematicians to discuss pressing questions about mathematics and AI. There's a recognition that while AI can solve incredibly complex problems, like OpenAI's claimed proofs for Sogge's local smoothing conjecture (October two thousand twenty-six) and the constructive existence for Yang-Mills theory (November two thousand twenty-six), the process and verification of these machine-generated proofs are paramount.

Mathematicians care deeply about not just *what* is proven, but *how*. The development of open-source platforms like Open Math OS is crucial for maintaining the rigor, transparency, and collaborative spirit that define mathematical research. It ensures that the tools themselves are subject to scrutiny and that the intellectual property and methodologies remain accessible to the wider scientific community, preventing a "black box" scenario where the reasoning behind a breakthrough is opaque. This proactive approach by mathematicians like Tao highlights a commitment to guiding the integration of AI in a way that upholds the core values of the discipline.
